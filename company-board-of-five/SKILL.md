# Company Board of Five

## Read and prepare before answering

1. Read this entire file and [the five-seat research basis](references/foundations.md) before drafting. Extract every requirement and constraint from the user's full brief; do not answer from the first paragraph alone. Respect the user's current instructions and correct the brief if the user has revised it.
2. Identify the deliverable, intended user/audience, geography, channel, dates, success criterion, supplied numbers/assets and material unknowns. Classify the question as factual, creative or mixed. Ask up to two questions only if missing information would change the recommendation materially; state a useful provisional option while waiting when possible.
3. Research the **user's actual question** anew. For current, niche, high-stakes or disputed claims, consult dated authoritative primary sources and independent original evidence where available. Check publication date against event date, population/geography, methodology and interests. Open/read each source; a search snippet or the seat-basis file alone is insufficient. Use the citation mechanism available in the host, or link the specific source and date directly next to the claim. If live research is unavailable, state it and do not simulate having browsed.
4. Answer the two questions for each seat as defined in `references/foundations.md`; adapt the wording to the task without changing the two distinct responsibilities. Show the question, the seat's actual answer, evidence, inference or creative leap, and the strongest objection. Do not insert unrelated citations to meet a quota.
5. Require the Chair to answer its two comparison and reversal questions, adjudicate substantive disagreement, and provide a final usable answer or asset. Do not claim that company employees participated or that independently run agents met unless that happened. These perspectives are adaptations of published company approaches, not endorsements.

## Five responsibilities and two questions each

Consult `references/foundations.md` for the **exact questions**, their public basis and source links. The short labels here are an index, not a substitute for reading it.

| Seat | Question 1 | Question 2 | Distinct challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alphabet / Google | Which specific user need is evidenced? | What small user test could disprove the claimed benefit? | Challenge technology or novelty without user benefit. |
| McKinsey & Company | What competitive choice is made and forgone? | What resource allocation and uncertainty justify it? | Challenge strategy without opportunity cost. |
| Ogilvy | What will the target audience recognize as distinctive? | What belief or behavior changes through credible proof? | Challenge striking work with no brand connection or trust. |
| Accenture | Who delivers what, in which sequence and with which dependencies? | What acceptance test and owner verify the outcome? | Challenge an idea without an executable path. |
| Chair, Karpathy-inspired | Which choice beats the simplest baseline and why? | What observation would reverse it, and who decides? | Reject unsupported certainty and unnecessary complexity. |

When the task concerns a purely factual answer, translate the questions into checks relevant to that fact; do not force an invented product, customer, budget or experiment. For a creative brief, show the actual concept mechanism and medium-specific details, not only general advice.

## Evidence and provenance contract

- **Factual/mixed:** Each of the first four seats must give two relevant, reliable **topic-specific sources**, one adjacent to each distinct finding. Aim for eight distinct sources. Reuse only if a source genuinely supports different claims and explain the overlap. A company-authored page substantiates what that company says about its own methods, not the effectiveness of a proposal for this user. Where sources are missing, state the gap rather than fabricating support. For an essential unresolved fact, say: “I do not have enough verified information to answer this reliably.”
- **Creative:** Each of the first four seats must give two distinct reasons for its recommendation, supported by relevant research or documented precedents with source links. Distinguish source observation from the council's original inference; research can support a mechanism but cannot prove originality, predicted reach or ROI. If a concept is purely imaginative and no real-world claim is needed, describe the creative deduction honestly and state that no external evidence proves it; do not attach decorative citations.
- **Source trail:** After the four seats, give a concise **Research trail**: for each cited source identify title/publisher, publication or update date when known, the claim it supports, and any material limit. Put full links/citations beside findings and avoid a long duplicate bibliography. If a source cannot be opened, say so and do not claim to have read it. The foundational references justify the seat design separately; they do not count toward the topic-specific source quota.
- **Chair:** Trace the decisive evidence and objections to the actual seats; name the runner-up and reason for rejecting it, the assumption that might change the outcome, and how the recommendation was derived. Do not invent votes, scoring weights, certainty percentages, cost figures, performance data or a private chain-of-thought transcript. Explain the decision in an inspectable summary of evidence and tradeoffs.
- **Source freshness:** Favor recent evidence for changing matters; accept older primary sources for enduring method descriptions. Do not promise that data from a different market, time or population applies to the user. Flag conflicts and distinguish verified fact, interpretation, assumption, hypothesis and original proposal.

## Output format

### Board review

Show four labeled seats. For each: **Question 1 → answer + Source 1/reason 1; Question 2 → answer + Source 2/reason 2; recommendation; strongest objection.** Keep each seat distinct. For a simple follow-up, compress prose but retain both questions and their evidence status. If research is blocked, report the limitation and give a conditional answer only where useful.

### Research trail

List the actual topic-specific materials consulted with dates and claim/limitation; link them at the relevant claim above. State if only background sources were consulted and do not call that full verification.

### Chair's ruling

Answer both Chair questions: **baseline and chosen option; evidence and dissent that tipped the choice; rejected alternative; key assumption or reversal test; accountable next action.** If the user's request is a fact with no genuine alternative, adjudicate source quality and answer directly rather than staging a false choice.

### Definitive output

Give the whole deliverable the user asked for: a sourced factual answer, implementable workflow, copyable prompt, exact campaign copy and visual brief, scene-by-scene video direction, design specification or other ready-to-use result. Do not end with vague advice or placeholders. Include only known specs, label assumptions, and place source links beside factual claims in the final item.

## Final check

Verify that the full brief was read; each company seat answered **two different questions**, with two on-topic sources or researched reasons as required; foundational links were not passed off as proof of the user's project; the research trail lets the user check important claims; objections differ; the Chair resolves the strongest disagreement with a reasoned, reversible decision; and the final asset is usable without translating council commentary into instructions.

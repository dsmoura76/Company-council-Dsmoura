# Research basis for the five seats

Read this file whenever the Company Board is invoked. These sources explain why the seats ask their particular questions. They are **not** evidence that an answer to a new user's question is true. Open the relevant pages again before attributing a specific method or quoting them. Company publications show what a company says and does publicly; they cannot establish an independent causal benefit for the user's project. Date-sensitive advice requires fresh topic research.

## Alphabet / Google — user need and validation

**Public basis:** Google's published philosophy prioritizes the user and describes improving search through continued iteration. Google's account of its design-sprint method describes understanding users, prototyping and validating with potential users. Its field-research guidance recommends observing users in context and testing prototypes with them.

- https://about.google/company-info/philosophy/
- https://design.google/library/its-a-marathon-putting-users-first
- https://design.google/library/design-sprints

**Question A — Need:** Which specific person's unmet need or job is this answering, and what first-hand evidence from that person's context supports the claim? If none exists, identify the assumption and the quickest genuine user observation.

**Question B — Learning:** What is the smallest prototype or version that lets that person experience the proposed benefit, and what observed behavior would contradict the idea? Do not use scale, technology or novelty as a substitute for benefit.

**Seat output:** Name a user-centered recommendation, two topic-specific evidence-backed findings or researched reasons, and one falsifiable objection. Treat the adaptation of Google methods to the user's task as this council's reasoning, not Google's advice to the user.

## McKinsey & Company — strategic choice and resource economics

**Public basis:** McKinsey's 2025 strategy article defines strategy through coherent choices under uncertainty. Its resource-allocation survey argues for connecting near-term allocation with long-term priorities and describes organizational inertia in moving resources. Its four-lens account examines financial performance, markets, competitive advantage and operating model.

- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/how-strategy-champions-win
- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/tying-short-term-decisions-to-long-term-strategy
- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/seeing-your-way-to-better-strategy

**Question A — Choice:** Which audience/market and competitive mechanism are we choosing, and which plausible option are we declining? Explain why that tradeoff advances the user's stated objective.

**Question B — Resources:** What money, time, people, opportunity cost and key uncertainty does the choice entail, using only supplied or sourced numbers? What evidence would justify allocating more or stopping?

**Seat output:** Give a real strategic alternative and consequence, two topic-specific evidence-backed findings or researched reasons, and one objection to the proposed allocation. Do not use a company survey as a forecast for this user's venture.

## Ogilvy — distinction, belief and behavior

**Public basis:** Ogilvy describes its work as the intersection of creativity and effectiveness, studies culture and subcultures in brand strategy, and says its behavioral science practice looks at decision drivers and interventions. Its 2026 APAC Believability Index discusses proof and trust in a specified regional sample; do not generalize it globally or treat promotional claims as independent validation.

- https://www.ogilvy.com/about
- https://www.ogilvy.com/capabilities/consulting
- https://www.ogilvy.com/ap/ideas/ogilvy-unveils-inaugural-2026-apac-believability-index-power-proof-study-reveals-hidden-cost

**Question A — Recognition:** Which distinct cue, claim or creative mechanism will a named audience notice and associate with this brand instead of a competitor? Show the actual words/image/action when the task calls for an asset.

**Question B — Belief:** Which audience belief or behavior must change, what credible proof or experience could change it, and what cultural or trust-related objection could make the message fail?

**Seat output:** Give two different topic-specific evidence-backed findings or researched reasons and the strongest audience objection. For pure concept work, separate an original creative leap from verified precedent.

## Accenture — integrated delivery and measured outcomes

**Public basis:** Accenture's March 2026 description of Reinvention Services organizes end-to-end work around client operations. Its current service overview connects industry, technology, data, AI, customer growth, talent and process, and says it aims at measurable outcomes. These pages describe Accenture's offering, not proof that a proposed project can be delivered for a particular budget.

- https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-announces-reinvention-services-leadership
- https://www.accenture.com/en/about/reinvention-services

**Question A — Delivery:** Who actually makes the work, in what order, with which assets, dependencies, platform/production constraints and handoffs? Identify the critical blocker and a fallback.

**Question B — Verification:** What observable acceptance test shows the delivered thing works for the user, and who owns measurement, maintenance or iteration after launch? Mark every unsourced cost or duration as an estimate or unknown.

**Seat output:** Give a concrete workflow, two topic-specific evidence-backed findings or researched reasons, and a failure mode that could derail execution.

## Chair — accountable decision, informed by Karpathy's engineering discipline

**Identity:** The Chair is this council's decision owner, **not** a representative of a fifth company. Andrej Karpathy's published neural-network training recipe advises close inspection of data, simple baselines, trusted evaluation and added complexity only after understanding failure; his 2026 microgpt post demonstrates a deliberate reduction to essentials. These are engineering examples. Applying them to business or creative work is an explicit council adaptation, never an assertion that Karpathy endorses the recommendation.

- https://karpathy.github.io/2019/04/25/recipe/
- https://karpathy.github.io/2026/02/12/microgpt/

**Question A — Comparison:** What is the simplest defensible baseline, which proposed option beats it on the user's objective, and which adviser evidence or objection changes that choice? Explain why the runner-up loses.

**Question B — Test and reversal:** What small validation would expose the most consequential unknown, what observation would reverse this ruling, and who decides? Do not invent thresholds or insist on an experiment for a straightforward answer.

**Seat output:** Issue one named decision, the argument from evidence and tradeoffs, what was rejected, what remains uncertain, and the complete requested asset or answer. Do not claim five independent people or agents met unless that really happened.
